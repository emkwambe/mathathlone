# Guide 5 — Family and Classroom Helper Guide

**Use this guide for:** explaining the managed Mathlete process to families, providing appropriate in-class help, and protecting student privacy during the pilot.

> The teacher, not the student, prepares the managed classroom account. The classroom workflow uses the minimum information needed for pilot competition and does not place a personal student email on the login card.[1]

## 1. What families should know

For the current classroom pilot, the teacher may create a managed Mathathlone account for an enrolled student. The student receives a private card containing a managed username and a temporary PIN. The card is intended for entry to the teacher’s class-bound Heat; it is not a social profile, a public ranking account, or a request for a personal email address.[1]

| Topic | Plain-language explanation |
|---|---|
| Student information | The teacher uses a school-approved display name and class roster relationship, not a student’s personal email on the login card. |
| Credentials | The username and temporary PIN are private. The student should not share them with classmates. |
| Classroom access | The student can join only the Heat tied to the class in which they are actively rostered. |
| Teacher help | The teacher can replace a lost temporary PIN for that individual student. The old PIN stops working. |
| Results | The initial rehearsal uses results as learning and operational feedback, not as a public or high-stakes cross-school ranking. |

## 2. Before a student participates

The school and teacher remain responsible for the normal local approvals, consent processes, device access, accommodations, and supervision practices. The application does not treat a self-checked online box as proof of parental consent. If the school needs a particular consent process before using a student’s information, complete that process before roster import.[1]

| Family or helper action | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Confirm the student may participate under the school’s normal process | Keeps the pilot aligned with local policy. |
| Review the private login-card rule with the student | Helps prevent accidental sharing of credentials. |
| Identify accessibility or device needs early | Lets the teacher plan appropriate support before the Heat opens. |
| Ask the teacher, not another student, for access help | Preserves the roster and account boundary. |

## 3. Helping at sign-in time

A classroom helper may help a student find the correct Heat link and enter the username/PIN from that student’s own card. Do not read cards aloud, photograph cards, collect cards into a shared list, or copy PINs into email or chat.

| Student need | Appropriate support | Not appropriate |
|---|---|---|
| Cannot find the Heat | Help open the teacher-provided link or ask the teacher for the code | Guessing codes or opening another class’s Heat |
| Cannot enter the PIN | Ask the teacher to verify the current card or reset only that student’s PIN | Borrowing a classmate’s card or sharing a PIN |
| Shows not enrolled | Tell the teacher before the Heat begins | Creating a duplicate account or changing another student’s class |
| Needs an accommodation | Follow the teacher’s and school’s approved plan | Altering competition settings without the teacher |

## 4. During the Heat

Students complete their own work. Helpers and families should follow the school’s expectations for assessment support. Do not provide answers, search for solutions, share questions, or help one student communicate with another during an active Heat.

| Moment | Helpful action |
|---|---|
| Student is waiting in lobby | Confirm they see **“You’re in”** and wait for the teacher to start. |
| Heat is active | Provide only the pre-approved accessibility or technical support; protect the student’s independent work. |
| Student finishes | Follow the teacher’s instructions and avoid discussing questions with students still working. |
| Results appear | Treat them as feedback and bring questions to the teacher after the session. |

## 5. If a card is lost or a student cannot continue

Tell the teacher privately. The teacher can issue a new temporary PIN for that student; the prior PIN stops working immediately. There is no current student self-service password recovery workflow for managed classroom accounts, so students should not attempt to register a second account during a Heat.[1]

| Do this | Do not do this |
|---|---|
| Ask the teacher for a targeted PIN reset | Reuse another student’s username or PIN |
| Use the newest card issued by the teacher | Keep using a PIN that has been reset |
| Report the exact on-screen message without including the PIN | Send a screenshot that exposes a credential |
| Wait for a future Heat if the current Heat already started | Join late through another account |

## 6. Recognition and respectful competition

The pilot supports students learning and competing at an appropriate level. Review content from a lower grade can be useful at the start of a school year, while students who demonstrate readiness for above-grade work deserve meaningful recognition. In either case, a student’s grade cohort remains important for fair peer comparison. The model rehearsal itself is not a formal cross-school ranking event.[2]
